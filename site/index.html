What is Minerva?

Know what a lot really costs - before you bid.

A hammer price of 500 is never 500. Add the buyer's premium, VAT, shipping, import VAT, the bank's cut on the exchange rate - and the lot you were about to bid on costs a third more, in a currency you don't pay in.

Minerva does that arithmetic for you, right on the auction page. Next to every price it shows what you would actually pay, in your own currency, updated live while the bids come in. Enter an auction's costs once, and every lot of that auction - and of that house - speaks your numbers. No spreadsheets, no calculator in a second tab, no surprise when the invoice arrives.

Features

  • Effective price inserted next to each price on the page
  • Live updates while bids change, also on in-page navigation
  • Conversion into several currencies (ECB reference rates)
  • Auction premium, shipment split over lots won, payment costs
  • Advanced: VAT on the hammer price, import VAT, flat fees
  • Settings saved per auction, auction house or site
  • Price breakdown pop-up on click
  • Output in the page's own price notation
  • Side panel (Chrome) / sidebar (Firefox)
  • All data stored locally in the browser; no account, no tracking

Supported sites

  • biddr.com
  • numisbids.com
  • l5.com

Supported currencies

  • EUR
  • USD
  • GBP
  • CHF

More sites and currencies to come in the future. If you want have suggestions, please let me know!

How to use it

1. Open an auction and the panel

Install Minerva and open a lot or auction on a supported site. The toolbar icon shows ON while Minerva is active on the page. Click it to open the side panel (Chrome) or sidebar (Firefox). At the top the panel shows the auction house and auction it has recognised, below them the settings form, and further down the Plugin status: how many prices were found and the exchange rate the page needs.

2. Enter the auction's costs

Your currency
The currency you pay in. All effective prices are shown in it.
Auction premium
Everything charged as a percentage of the hammer price, added up into one number: the buyer's premium including its VAT (e.g. 20 % premium + 19 % VAT = 23.8 % auction premium) plus any online or live bidding surcharge.
Shipment
Shipping and insurance per invoice, in the auction's currency (shown next to the input).
Lots won
How many lots you expect to win. Shipment and flat fees are charged once per invoice, so each lot carries its share.
Payment costs
Your bank's or card's markup on the exchange rate (often 1-3 %) plus any surcharge for the payment method.
Advanced cost settings
A switch revealing hammer price VAT (regularly taxed or imported lots), import VAT (lots shipped in from outside your customs area, e.g. 7 % for collector coins in Germany) and flat fees (customs clearance, transfer fees, in your currency). It is switched on automatically when one of them is set for the auction. While it is off, these costs count as 0.

Every input has a ? next to its label that shows a short explanation. The Save button turns solid while there are unsaved changes; invalid values are marked and nothing is saved. An emptied input keeps the value that was shown. After saving, the page recalculates immediately, and so do other open tabs of the same auction.

3. How settings are saved and found again

Minerva tries to read the site, the auction house and the auction from the page address and saves your settings under all three. When you open a page, it uses the most specific settings it has:

  1. Auction - settings saved for this very auction,
  2. House - otherwise the latest saved for the same auction house,
  3. Site - otherwise the latest saved on the same site,
  4. Defaults - otherwise no costs at all, in EUR.

The panel highlights which of these the values come from. So the premium you entered for one auction of a house is a good starting point for its next one - adjust and save, and the new auction has its own. Where the address does not name the house (numisbids.com) or the auction (lot pages on l5.com), that level is skipped. Unrecognised IDs are shown as "unknown".

4. Read the prices

Each price on the page gets a Minerva bubble right next to it with the effective price: 

lot   = hammer × (1 + premium% + hammer VAT%) + shipment ÷ lots won
total = lot in your currency × (1 + payment costs% + import VAT%) + flat fees ÷ lots won
  • Click a bubble to see how this price adds up, part by part. While the panel holds unsaved values for this auction, the pop-up already uses them - handy for trying out a premium before saving. The bubbles keep the saved values until you save.
  • Prices are written the way the page writes them - currency position, decimal and grouping separators: 1.234,56 EUR comes out like 1.334,56 EUR, $49.99 like €75.00.
  • A bubble shows n/a when the text holds no price, or its currency is unknown or has no exchange rate yet. Minerva never guesses.
  • Exchange rates are the ECB reference rates from Frankfurter, saved with your settings and refreshed daily. They are mid-market rates - that is what payment costs is for.

Privacy

Settings and rates stay in your browser's extension storage. The only request Minerva makes is for exchange rates, to the Frankfurter API; it does not read anything but the prices on supported sites.

Bugs & feature requests

Found a wrong price, a page Minerva does not pick up, or a site it should support? Please open an issue on GitHub - with the page address, what you expected and what Minerva showed. Feature requests are welcome there, too.

About me

I collect coins and bid on auctions across Europe, the UK and the US. Every house charges differently, every site shows a different currency, and working out what a lot would really cost me meant a spreadsheet next to every catalogue. Minerva is the tool I wanted to have: the true price, where the bidding happens.
